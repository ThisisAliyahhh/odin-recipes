Gbegiri

Gbegiri is a Nigerian bean soup commonly known to the Yoruba people. It is usually eaten with Amala or Ewedu.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup brown beans or black-eyed peas (peeled)
  • 1/4 cup palm oil (just enough to give it a light orange tint)
  • 1 medium onion (chopped)
  • 1 tablespoon fermented locust beans (iru or dawadawa, optional)
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons ground crayfishGround pepper or chili powder (to taste)
  • Bouillon cubes / seasoning (1 to 2 cubes)
  • Salt (to taste)
  • Water

Steps

  1. Prep and Peel the Beans
  2. Soak your beans in water for 3 to 5 minutes until the skin swells slightly.
  3. Peel the skin off by rubbing them between your hands or pulsing them briefly in a food processor with water.
  4. Rinse multiple times until the beans are completely clean and free of shafts.
  5. Cook Until SoftTransfer the peeled beans into a pot and add the chopped onion and enough water to cover them.
  6. Bring to a boil and cook on medium heat for 30 to 45 minutes until the beans are extremely soft and easily mashed. (A pressure cooker cuts this time down to about 25 minutes).
  7. Blend or MashTurn off the heat and let it cool slightly.Pour the cooked beans into a blender and blend until completely smooth.
  8. Alternative: Use a traditional local broom (ijabe) to mash the beans smooth in the pot, then pass the paste through a sieve to remove any remaining lumps.
  9. Season and Simmer
  10. Pour the smooth bean puree back into the pot over low heat.
  11. Add a little water if the paste is too thick; the final consistency should be semi-fluid and light.
  12. Add the palm oil, ground crayfish, fermented locust beans (iru), pepper, seasoning cubes, and salt.
  13. Stir well and let it simmer for 5 to 7 minutes so the palm oil integrates completely and the raw taste cooks out.
  14. Remove from heat once you reach your desired smooth, pouring consistency.
  15. Serve your hot Gbegiri soup traditionally alongside Amala and Ewedu soup (often combined as Abula), topped with your choice of meat or fish stew.
