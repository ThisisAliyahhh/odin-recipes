Eba

Eba is a swallow from Nigeria, Togo and Benin in the West African sub-region and other African countries. The term Ẹ̀bà originates from Yoruba. It is a cooked starchy vegetable food made from dried grated cassava flour commonly known as Garri all across West Africa. It is often eaten with rich soups and stews, with beef, stockfish or mutton. It is eaten with the fingers, rolled into a small ball, and dipped into thick soups such as okra soup, chilli paste in Togo, bitter leaf soup or with either okro, ọgbọnọ/apọn, or ewédú, meat or fish, stewed vegetables or other sauces such as gbẹ̀gìrì, ofe akwu (banga soup) or egusi soup.

Ingredients

  • Garri: 1 to 2 cups (white or yellow garri)
  • Water: 2 to 3 cups of freshly boiled, hot water
  • Tools: A pot or kettle to boil water, a mixing bowl or pot, and a wooden spatula or turning spoon (omorogun)

Steps

  1. Boil the water: Heat water in a pot or kettle until it comes to a rolling boil.
  2. Pour into a bowl or keep on low heat: Pour the boiling water into a large heatproof bowl, or turn off the heat if using a pot on the stove.
  3. Sprinkle the garri: Gradually sprinkle the garri evenly over the surface of the hot water. Do not dump it all in one spot to prevent lumps.
  4. Let it rest: Allow the garri to absorb the hot water undisturbed for about 3 to 5 minutes.
  5. Turn and mix: Use a wooden spoon or spatula to vigorously mix, press, and turn the dough until it becomes smooth, soft, and completely lump-free.
  6. Molding and serving: Shape the eba into a smooth ball or portion sizes, and serve hot with your favorite Nigerian soup like egusi or okra soup.
